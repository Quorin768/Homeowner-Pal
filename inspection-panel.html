Challenger / FPE / Zinsco panel

Inspectors flag Challenger, Federal Pacific (Stab-Lok), and Zinsco panels on sight. They look like normal gray breaker panels, the brand label is the giveaway.

Realistic cost range
$1,500 to $4,000
Price range is for an interior panel swap. Exterior panels (panel mounted outside, often combined with the meter) cost more, the meter base, weatherproof enclosure, and utility coordination add to the job. Final cost depends on circuit count, service size, location, and whether the meter base needs upgrading.

Heads up, drywall damage is likely. Pulling new wire or replacing a panel often requires opening walls and ceilings. Most electricians do not patch or paint drywall as part of the quote. Budget separately for drywall repair and paint, or hire a handyman afterward.

Why inspectors flag this
  • Independent testing has shown failure-to-trip rates between 25% and 80% depending on brand and panel age, a breaker that doesn't trip is the cause of an electrical fire, not a prevention.
  • Insurance companies in many states will not write a policy on a home with one of these panels.
  • The bus bars (the metal strips breakers clip onto) are known to fail and arc, the panel itself becomes the failure point.
  • Replacement parts are no longer manufactured for many of these panels, so even repairs are workarounds.

Important, please read

HomeownerPal (HOP) is built and maintained by people with decades of combined hands-on trade experience. The guidance here is grounded in real field experience and is intended to help homeowners understand what's likely happening, what a fair price range looks like, and what questions to ask. That said, home systems are complex and every situation is different. Some issues simply require a qualified technician on-site to physically test, locate, and confirm the actual cause before any repair should be made.

Electricity, gas, and water systems can cause serious injury or death. If you smell burning, see smoke or sparks, hear sustained buzzing, or otherwise suspect an emergency, stop, leave the area, and call 911 and/or a licensed professional immediately. Do not attempt repairs you are not trained or permitted to perform; many jurisdictions legally require licensed and inspected work.
