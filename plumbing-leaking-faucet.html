Dripping or leaking faucet? What's causing it.

A faucet drips when off, or leaks at the base or handle.

The location of the leak identifies the part. Dripping from the spout points to the cartridge or washer. Water at the base points to O-rings. Water under the sink is a supply line, shutoff, or P-trap, and that's the one to deal with quickly before it damages the cabinet.

Step 1

Dripping or leaking faucet

Where is the water coming from?

Points to a worn cartridge or washer.

The internal seal that stops water flow has worn out. Look up your faucet brand and model, a matching cartridge kit is almost always available.

Usually a worn O-ring.

The O-ring or base gasket on the faucet body has failed. This is a fairly quick fix once you have the matching parts for your faucet's brand and model.

Deal with this one quickly, it can damage the cabinet.

This points to a supply line, a shutoff valve, or the P-trap rather than the faucet itself. Close the angle stops under the sink first to stop the water, then find the exact source before it gets worse.

Likely the stem seal or upper cartridge gasket.

This wears out from the same use as the spout cartridge, often the same repair kit covers both.

Common causes of this problem

  • Worn faucet cartridge or washer
    Internal seal in the faucet has worn out
  • Leak at the baseLikely a worn O-ring
    O-ring or base gasket on the faucet body has failed
  • Leak at the handle when running
    Stem seal or upper cartridge gasket worn out
  • Leaking supply line or shutoff valve
    Failed braided supply line, packing nut, or angle stop
  • Leaking P-trap under the sink
    Loose slip-nut washer or cracked plastic P-trap
  • Find the source before it gets bigger
    Slow leak somewhere under the sink, supply or drain side

Safe things a homeowner can check

  • Close the angle stops (the small valves under the sink) by turning them clockwise. Turn the faucet on to confirm no water comes out before taking anything apart.

  • Look up your faucet brand and model, the manufacturer almost always sells a matching cartridge kit, and a 2-minute YouTube search will show the exact disassembly steps for your faucet.

  • Plug the drain with a rag so dropped parts don't disappear. Pop off the handle cap, remove the screw, lift the handle, and pull the cartridge straight up. Bring the old cartridge to a plumbing supply store or hardware store to match it.

  • Reverse the steps with the new cartridge. Open the angle stops slowly and check for drips at every connection.

  • Shut off the angle stops under the sink.

  • Identify the faucet brand and search for the model's repair kit, it almost always includes both the cartridge and the O-rings.

  • Remove the spout (some lift straight up, some require unscrewing a small set screw), replace the O-rings, lightly grease them with silicone plumber's grease before reassembly.

  • Shut off the angle stops, then look up the faucet brand and model and order the matching cartridge kit.

When to stop and call a professional

  1. If the angle stops under the sink are corroded, frozen, or start leaking when you try to close them, stop, those need to be replaced first, and that's a good moment to bring in a plumber.

  2. If the faucet is old, generic, or you can't find a matching cartridge, it's often cheaper to replace the whole faucet than to chase parts.

  3. If you reassemble and the drip continues, the seat inside the faucet body may be pitted, replacement is usually the move.

  4. If the faucet is older than 10 to 15 years and parts are hard to find, replacement is usually faster and cheaper than chasing a repair.

  5. If the angle stops won't close or leak when you touch them, call a plumber to replace those first.

  6. If a replacement cartridge doesn't stop the leak, the faucet body itself may be corroded and the faucet should be replaced.

  7. Bad or corroded angle stops should be replaced by a plumber, they often require shutting off the water at the main and sometimes soldering, depending on the connection.

  8. If you see green corrosion, swelling, or staining at the supply line connection, replace before it lets go.
