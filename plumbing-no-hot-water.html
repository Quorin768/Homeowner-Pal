Plumbing diagnosis

No hot water, or it runs out too fast? What to check.

No hot water at the taps, lukewarm only, or the shower goes cold mid-rinse.

Start by identifying whether your water heater is electric, gas, or tankless, the causes are completely different. If you smell gas, leave and call the gas utility from outside. If the tank itself is leaking, shut off water and power and call a plumber.

Step 1

No hot water, or it runs out fast

First, any gas smell, smoke, or water around the water heater?

Walk to the water heater (garage, basement, utility closet) and look at the floor and the top of the tank. Don't touch hot pipes.

Get out now, this is a real emergency.

  1. Do not flip light switches, plug/unplug anything, or use your phone inside.
  2. Get everyone, including pets, out of the house. Leave the door open behind you.
  3. From outside, call your gas utility's 24/7 emergency line (on your gas bill, or dial 811).
  4. If you see fire or hear loud hissing, call 911 from outside.
  5. Do not go back in until the utility says it's safe.

Shut it off and call a plumber, same-day priority.

Shut off the cold-water valve on top of the tank (turn clockwise). If you can't reach it, shut off the main water supply to the house. Then cut power at the breaker (electric) or turn the gas valve to "off" or "pilot" (gas). Call a plumber and describe it as a leaking water heater.

Good, this is a troubleshooting situation, not an emergency.

Figure out whether your heater is electric, gas, or tankless, the fix path is different for each. See the causes and checks below.

Safe things a homeowner can check

  • Check the electrical panel for a tripped breaker labeled "water heater" (usually a 30A double-pole). If you find one, reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.

  • If the breaker is fine, look on the side of the tank for a small access panel near the top. Switch the breaker off before opening it. Inside there's insulation, a thermostat, and a small red reset button. Press it firmly, if it clicks, you've found the issue. Reinstall everything and turn the breaker back on.

  • If the reset trips again within a day, the upper thermostat or an element is likely bad, that's a pro repair, not a DIY for most homeowners because of the 240V wiring and the need to drain the tank to swap an element.

  • Read the relight instructions on the label, every brand is a little different.

  • Turn the dial to "OFF" and wait 5 minutes for any unburned gas to clear before trying to relight. Don't skip this, it's what prevents a small puff.

  • Follow the printed pilot-light steps exactly: usually "PILOT" setting, hold down the dial or red button, click the ignitor several times until it lights, then keep the button held for 30 to 60 seconds before releasing and turning to "ON."

  • If the pilot won't ignite or won't stay lit after release, stop and call a plumber. Don't keep cycling the gas.

  • Once or twice a year, draining a few gallons from the bottom drain valve into a bucket flushes loose sediment. Search "how to flush a water heater" for your specific brand and follow the procedure carefully, the water coming out is hot.

When to stop and call a professional

  1. Do not flip light switches, plug or unplug anything, or use anything that could spark, gas leaks ignite from sparks, not just open flames.

  2. Get everyone (including pets) out of the house. Leave the door open behind you.

  3. From outside, call your gas utility's 24/7 emergency line, the number is on your gas bill, or you can dial 811. Most utilities respond at no charge.

  4. If you see fire or hear loud hissing, call 911 from outside.

  5. Do not go back in until the utility says it's safe.

  6. Shut off the cold-water valve on top of the tank by turning it clockwise. If you can't reach it or it won't turn, shut off the main water supply to the house.

  7. For a gas heater, turn the dial on top of the gas valve to "OFF" or "PILOT." For an electric heater, switch off its breaker in the panel.

  8. Call a plumber and describe it as a leaking water heater, most will treat it as same-day work.
